Why points win here

Each Chase point is worth about 2.6¢ on this trip — a strong redemption. Anything over 2¢ per point typically beats cashing them out, and this one's well above the line.

For reference: the same 60,000 points cashed out at Chase's base rate would only be worth $600. Using them for this flight gets you $1,560 of value.

How to book this
  1. 1. Transfer 60,000 Chase points to United (instant, 1:1). Chase Ultimate Rewards is your bank points currency; "transfer" means moving them to United.
  2. 2. Book the flight on united.com using your United miles. Search by date, look for "Saver" award availability.
  3. 3. Pay the ~$80 in taxes & fees at checkout. Those aren't coverable by points on any US carrier.
What's a "transfer partner"?

Banks like Chase, Amex, and Citi let you move their points to partner airlines and hotels. Chase Ultimate Rewards transfers to United at a 1-to-1 ratio, instantly, so 60,000 Chase points becomes 60,000 United miles.

This is why banks issue their own points — they're more flexible than being locked into one airline.

How much cash is 60,000 points worth?

It depends how you use them. Cashed out directly from Chase, 60,000 points = $600. Redeemed through the Chase travel portal, they're worth $750–$900. Transferred to United for this flight, they're getting you $1,560 of value.

That's why this is a good use. Same points, but the transfer gets you 2–3× what cashing out would.

Why cash wins here

Using 28,000 points to avoid a $318 fare means each point is only working for you at about 1.1¢ of value. Most point enthusiasts aim for 2¢ or higher — below 1.5¢ is considered a waste.

Rule of thumb: points give the most value on long-haul, premium cabin, or peak-date trips. Short domestic flights like this are almost always better in cash.

When would points be worth it on this route?

Points start making sense on JFK–MIA when cash fares spike above ~$500 round trip — typically spring break, Thanksgiving week, or Christmas. At those prices, 28,000 points would be worth roughly 1.8¢ each, closer to a reasonable trade.
