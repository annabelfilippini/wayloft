## 1. How Travel Sites Earn from Credit Card Recommendations

### The Basic Model

Credit card affiliate programs pay publishers (websites, apps, tools) a commission when a user clicks an affiliate link and is subsequently approved for a credit card. The transaction chain works as follows:

1. **User visits your site** and sees a credit card recommendation (e.g., "Best cards for earning airline miles")
2. **User clicks your affiliate link**, which contains a tracking ID unique to your site
3. **User is redirected** to the card issuer's application page (Chase, Amex, etc.)
4. **User applies for the card** and (usually) is approved
5. **The issuer confirms the approval** to the affiliate network
6. **The network pays you** a commission, typically 30-90 days after approval

### Revenue Models

There are three primary compensation structures in credit card affiliate marketing:

**Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) / Bounty Model:**
- You earn a fixed dollar amount per approved application
- This is the dominant model in credit card affiliate programs
- Typical range: $50-$200+ per approval depending on the card
- Premium cards (Amex Platinum, Chase Sapphire Reserve) pay the highest bounties
- You earn nothing if the user clicks but does not apply, or applies but is denied
- Approval rates for referred traffic typically range from 20-40%

**Cost Per Click (CPC) Model:**
- You earn a fixed amount per click on an affiliate link, regardless of whether the user applies
- Much lower per-click payouts (typically $0.50-$5.00 per click)
- Lower risk for publishers since you earn regardless of conversion
- Less common in credit card programs; more typical for comparison tools
- Some networks offer CPC for new publishers who haven't yet proven conversion quality

**Hybrid Model:**
- Combines a smaller CPC payment with a smaller CPA bonus on approval
- Example: $1.00 per click + $50 per approval
- Used by some networks to balance publisher and advertiser risk
- Often available as a stepping stone before full CPA access

**Revenue Per Click (EPC) -- The Key Metric:**
- EPC = total commissions / total clicks
- This is the metric that determines your actual revenue efficiency
- Industry average EPC for credit card affiliates: $15-$45
- Top performers with highly targeted traffic achieve $50-$100+ EPC
- Miles Optimizer should target $25-$40 EPC given its highly qualified audience

### Why Credit Card Affiliates Pay So Much

Credit card issuers are willing to pay $50-$200+ per approved customer because:
- **Customer lifetime value is enormous.** A single credit card customer generates $500-$2,000+ in revenue over the card's lifetime through interchange fees, interest charges, and annual fees
- **Acquisition cost benchmarks are high.** Direct mail campaigns cost $50-$100+ per account acquired; digital channels are comparable or cheaper
- **Travel-focused audiences are premium.** Users on sites like Miles Optimizer are high-income, high-spending customers -- exactly the demographic issuers want
- **Annual fee cards have immediate ROI.** When Amex acquires a Platinum cardholder at $695/year in annual fees, paying $200 in acquisition cost is highly profitable

## 2. Major Affiliate Networks for Credit Cards

### CardRatings

**Overview:** CardRatings (owned by Bankrate/Red Ventures since the Bankrate acquisition) is one of the oldest and most established credit card affiliate networks. They serve as an intermediary between card issuers and publishers.

**Key Details:**
- **How to apply:** Online application at CardRatings.com/partners or via direct outreach
- **Requirements:** Must have a live website with credit card or financial content; lower traffic minimums than direct issuer programs
- **Issuers available:** Chase, American Express, Capital One, Citi, Barclays, Bank of America, US Bank, Wells Fargo, Discover, and dozens of smaller issuers
- **Payment model:** Primarily CPA (per approval); some CPC options for newer publishers
- **Payment terms:** Net 60-90 days after month of approval
- **Minimum payout:** Typically $50-$100
- **Tracking:** Cookie-based with click-through tracking; some use first-party data
- **Support:** Dedicated account managers for larger publishers

**Pros:**
- Widest selection of card issuers in a single network
- Lower barrier to entry than direct issuer programs
- Established reputation and reliable payments
- Good reporting dashboard
- Often the first network new publishers should join

**Cons:**
- Commission rates may be 10-20% lower than direct issuer relationships
- You are one step removed from the issuer (less negotiating power)
- Approval can take 2-4 weeks
- Some high-value cards may have limited availability

**Best for Miles Optimizer:** Primary network to start with. Apply immediately.

5. **Network vs. direct:** Moving from a network to a direct relationship typically increases CPA by 10-30%

## 4. Compliance Requirements

### FTC Disclosure Requirements

The Federal Trade Commission requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of material relationships between publishers and the products they recommend.

**What must be disclosed:**
- That you earn a commission when users click your links and are approved for cards
- That your recommendations may be influenced by your compensation arrangements
- That your card rankings or "best of" lists may be affected by affiliate relationships

**How to disclose (FTC best practices):**
- **Placement:** Disclosures must be near the affiliate link or recommendation, not buried in a footer
- **Proximity:** The disclosure should be close enough to the claim that a reader naturally sees it
- **Prominence:** Use the same size, color, and contrast as the surrounding content
- **Language:** Plain, unambiguous English. "We earn a commission" is better than "affiliate partners"
- **Repeated disclosure:** If affiliate links appear in multiple sections, disclosure should appear in or near each section

**Example compliant disclosure language:**

> "Miles Optimizer may receive compensation when you click on links to credit card products on our site. This compensation may impact how and where products appear on this site (including the order in which they appear). Miles Optimizer does not include all credit card companies or all available credit card offers. Editorial opinions expressed on this site are our own and are not provided, reviewed, or endorsed by any credit card issuer."

### CARD Act Requirements

The Credit Card Accountability Responsibility and Disclosure Act of 2009 primarily regulates credit card issuers, not affiliates. However, certain provisions create obligations for how you present card information:

- **Fair comparison:** Do not misrepresent card terms (APR, fees, rewards rates)
- **Current information:** Card terms change frequently. Displaying outdated APRs or sign-up bonuses can create liability
- **Schumer Box:** When displaying detailed card terms, consider including or linking to the standardized Schumer Box disclosure

### Network-Specific Compliance Requirements

**Common network compliance rules:**
- **No incentivized clicks:** You cannot offer users rewards for clicking affiliate links
- **No misleading claims:** Cannot guarantee approval, specific credit limits, or bonus amounts
- **Brand guidelines:** Must use approved card images, logos, and names exactly as provided
- **Cookie/tracking requirements:** Cannot use cookie stuffing, forced clicks, or pop-under windows
- **PPC/SEM restrictions:** Most issuers prohibit affiliates from bidding on their brand keywords

### Miles Optimizer Compliance Checklist

| Requirement | Implementation | Priority |
|-------------|---------------|----------|
| FTC disclosure on every page with affiliate links | Site-wide disclosure banner + per-section disclosures | P0 |
| Accurate card terms (APR, fees, bonus) | Automated term verification system or manual quarterly audit | P0 |

| No misleading approval guarantees | Review all copy, implement editorial guidelines | P0 |

## 5. How Major Players Structure Their Programs

### The Points Guy (TPG)

**Overview:** The Points Guy is the gold standard for credit card affiliate monetization in the travel space. Founded by Brian Kelly in 2010, acquired by Red Ventures (owners of Bankrate, CreditCards.com) in 2012 for a reported $20M, now generating estimated $100M+ in annual revenue.

**Revenue model:**
- **Primary revenue (estimated 80%+): Credit card affiliate commissions.** TPG's entire content strategy is built around driving credit card applications.
- **Secondary revenue:** Display advertising (premium CPMs due to high-income audience), sponsored content partnerships with hotel/airline brands, events
- **Content strategy:** Massive SEO-driven content operation with 50+ full-time editorial staff. TPG publishes 20+ articles per day.

**Key tactics Miles Optimizer can learn from:**
1. **Card valuation methodology:** TPG publishes proprietary point valuations (e.g., "Chase Ultimate Rewards points are worth 2.0 cents each") that drive card recommendation logic
2. **"Best cards for X" content:** These comparison articles drive the majority of affiliate revenue
3. **Card recommendation widget:** Embedded throughout the site, recommending cards contextually
4. **Monthly "best offers" updates:** Creates urgency and repeat traffic
5. **YouTube/video:** Card unboxing and review videos drive significant traffic and trust

**What TPG does differently than Miles Optimizer could:**
- TPG is a content/media company first, tool second. Miles Optimizer is a tool first, content second.
- TPG's card recommendations are editorial. Miles Optimizer's can be algorithmic and personalized (better user experience, potentially higher conversion).
- TPG's audience is broad. Miles Optimizer targets power users who are more likely to apply for premium cards (higher CPAs).

## Appendix C: Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **CPA** | Cost Per Acquisition -- payment per approved card application |
| **CPC** | Cost Per Click -- payment per affiliate link click |
| **EPC** | Earnings Per Click -- total commissions divided by total clicks |
| **CTR** | Click-Through Rate -- percentage of users who click an affiliate link |
| **Net 60/90** | Payment terms -- you receive payment 60 or 90 days after the approval month |
| **Schumer Box** | Standardized disclosure table for credit card terms required by TILA |
| **5/24 Rule** | Chase's policy of generally denying applicants who have opened 5+ new credit cards in 24 months |
| **Churning** | The practice of repeatedly opening and closing credit cards to earn sign-up bonuses |
| **Transferable points** | Points currencies (Chase UR, Amex MR, Citi TYP, Capital One Miles, Bilt) that can be transferred to airline/hotel loyalty programs |
