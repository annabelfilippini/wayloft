## 1. Preliminary Mark Analysis

### Distinctiveness Spectrum

Trademarks are ranked on a spectrum from weakest to strongest:

1. **Generic** (unprotectable) -- e.g., "Travel Rewards"
2. **Descriptive** (protectable only with secondary meaning) -- e.g., "Miles Optimizer"
3. **Suggestive** (inherently protectable) -- e.g., "Expedia" (suggests expediting travel)
4. **Arbitrary** (strong) -- e.g., "Apple" for computers
5. **Fanciful/Coined** (strongest) -- e.g., "Zillow," "Spotify"

**"Wayloft" falls between suggestive and fanciful.** It is a coined compound of "Way" (journey, path, method) + "Loft" (elevated space, to lift up). The combination does not exist as a dictionary word. It subtly evokes travel ("way" as journey) and aspiration/elevation ("loft"), which is ideal: it suggests the product domain without describing it. This is significantly stronger than the previous name "Miles Optimizer," which was descriptive and harder to protect.

### Phonetic & Visual Analysis

- **Pronunciation:** WAY-loft (two syllables, easy to say)
- **Spelling:** Intuitive, no ambiguity
- **Phonetic equivalents to check:** Way Loft, Wailoft, Weyloft, Wayloft, Waylofts
- **Visual conflicts to consider:** WayLoft (CamelCase), Way-Loft (hyphenated)

### Comparison to the Old Name

| Factor | "Miles Optimizer" | "Wayloft" |
|--------|------------------|-----------|
| Distinctiveness | Descriptive (weakest protectable) | Coined/Fanciful (strongest) |
| Registrability | Difficult without secondary meaning | Strong -- likely registrable |
| Enforcement | Hard to enforce against similar descriptive marks | Easy to enforce -- unique coined word |
| Domain availability | Poor (common words, likely taken) | Better (coined word) |
| Long-term brand equity | Limited (sounds generic) | High (distinctive, memorable) |
